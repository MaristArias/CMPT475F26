# Prototype Demo: Expectations and Grading Rubric

**Total: 100 points**

## Purpose

Your demo should help the audience understand **what your completed system will look like, how people will use it, and what value it will provide.**

Teams may be at different stages of development. You may demonstrate working software, clickable prototypes, wireframes, storyboards, or clearly identified simulations. **You can earn full marks at any development stage** by presenting a concrete, coherent preview of your final system.

## Grading rubric

| Criterion | Points | What earns full credit |
|---|---:|---|
| **Final system vision** | **30** | Clearly identify your intended users, the problem you address, and the main capabilities of the completed system. Show representative screens, interfaces, or outputs so the audience can picture the final product. |
| **Demonstration of core workflows** | **30** | Walk through realistic scenarios showing how a user completes important tasks. Connect the essential steps from an initial need or input to a meaningful result. Clearly explain any simulated steps. |
| **User experience and design coherence** | **15** | Present understandable navigation, interactions, information, and feedback. Explain important design choices and how they support your intended users, including relevant usability and accessibility needs. |
| **Current status and path to completion** | **15** | Clearly distinguish what is implemented, simulated, and planned. Identify important gaps or uncertainties and explain your priorities for reaching the demonstrated final vision. |
| **Demo organization and explanation** | **10** | Deliver a prepared, focused walkthrough that is easy to follow. Explain what is happening and answer questions clearly and honestly. |
| **Total** | **100** | |

## How to prepare your demo

1. **Introduce the users and their problem.** Briefly explain who will use your system and what they need to accomplish.
2. **Show the intended experience.** Walk through representative tasks using realistic examples. Make the connections between screens, actions, and results visible.
3. **Explain the current state.** Identify working features, sample data, simulated responses, and planned functionality as they appear.
4. **Explain what comes next.** Identify the most important remaining work, major uncertainties, and immediate priorities.

For example, if your backend is unfinished, you can demonstrate the intended interaction using sample data and simulated responses. Explain what the user would do, what the system would return, and which parts still need implementation.

## How your work will be assessed

| Performance level | Description |
|---|---|
| **Excellent** | Concrete, coherent evidence makes the intended system and user workflows clear. Status and next steps are explicit. |
| **Proficient** | The intended system is understandable, with minor gaps in the demonstration or explanation. |
| **Developing** | The general idea is clear, but significant parts of the experience, workflow, or completion plan remain unclear. |
| **Limited** | The audience must infer much of the intended system because the demonstration provides insufficient evidence. |

These levels apply separately to each criterion. A criterion receives zero points when no assessable evidence is provided.

## Fairness across development stages

- **The same criteria and weights apply to every team.** The amount of completed code does not determine your score.
- **Clear wireframes can earn full design credit.** Production styling, animations, and branding are not required.
- **A concrete walkthrough provides stronger evidence than a promise.** Show how a planned feature will fit into the user experience.
- **Clearly disclosed simulation is acceptable.** Be accurate about what currently works.
- **Prepare a backup.** Screenshots, recordings, or an alternative prototype can preserve your demonstration if technical problems occur.

Full marks on this demo recognize a clear and credible preview of your completed system. Implementation, integration, testing, and deployment remain expectations for the final project.
