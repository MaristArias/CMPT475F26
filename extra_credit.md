# Extra Credit Presentation Topics

## CMPT 475 Capping

Students may give a short presentation on a topic relevant to modern computing practice. Topics may address an emerging technology, the development and operation of software systems, or the management of technical projects and teams.

The best presentations do more than define a term. They explain **why the topic matters**, demonstrate or analyze how it works, identify its limitations and trade-offs, and connect it to decisions a project team might face.

> Topics should be approved before presentation. Closely related topics may be combined or narrowed to fit the available presentation time.

## Emerging Technologies

1. **AI coding agents and agentic software development**  
   What can coding agents complete autonomously, and where must developers remain in control?

2. **Beyond “vibe coding”: turning AI-generated prototypes into reliable software**  
   What engineering work is required after an AI tool produces a convincing first version?

3. **Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)**  
   When should an AI system retrieve external knowledge, and how can its answers be evaluated?

4. **Agent protocols and tool interoperability**  
   How do standards such as the Model Context Protocol allow AI systems to use data and tools, and what new risks do they introduce?

5. **Small language models and on-device AI**  
   When are smaller or locally deployed models preferable to large cloud-hosted models?

6. **Multimodal AI systems**  
   How do applications combine text, images, audio, and video, and how should their output be tested?

7. **AI evaluation and observability**  
   How can teams measure hallucinations, quality, safety, latency, drift, and cost in production AI systems?

8. **Securing AI applications**  
   How do prompt injection, data poisoning, model theft, insecure tool use, and sensitive-data leakage affect system design?

9. **Synthetic data**  
   When can artificially generated data address scarcity or privacy concerns, and when can it distort results?

10. **Digital provenance, deepfakes, and content authenticity**  
    Can technical standards help people determine where digital content came from and whether it has been altered?

11. **Post-quantum cryptography**  
    Why must organizations migrate before cryptographically relevant quantum computers exist, and what makes migration difficult?

12. **Passkeys and the future of authentication**  
    How do passkeys improve on passwords, and what usability, recovery, and adoption challenges remain?

13. **Confidential computing and privacy-enhancing technologies**  
    How can systems process sensitive data while reducing exposure to infrastructure operators or other parties?

14. **WebAssembly beyond the browser**  
    Can WebAssembly provide a portable, secure runtime for cloud, edge, plug-in, or serverless applications?

15. **Edge AI and intelligent IoT systems**  
    When should inference happen near a device rather than in the cloud, and how does that affect privacy and reliability?

16. **Spatial computing, augmented reality, and digital twins**  
    Where do immersive or simulated environments create genuine value rather than novelty?

17. **Green software engineering**  
    How can developers measure and reduce the energy, carbon, and hardware costs of software and AI workloads?

18. **Modern language trends: TypeScript, Rust, Python, and Go**  
    What do current language choices reveal about safety, AI-assisted development, cloud systems, and developer productivity?

    How can a platform improve developer productivity without becoming another layer of bureaucracy?

20. **Cloud-native architecture for AI workloads**  
    How do containers, Kubernetes, accelerators, model serving, and data pipelines support production AI?

25. **Local-first and offline-first software**

26. **Observability: logs, metrics, traces, and OpenTelemetry**  
    What evidence does a team need to understand a distributed system in production?

27. **Site reliability engineering and error budgets**  
    How can teams balance feature delivery against availability, latency, and operational risk?

28. **Resilience engineering and chaos testing**  
    How can controlled failure experiments reveal weaknesses before real incidents occur?

29. **Continuous integration, continuous delivery, and progressive delivery**  
    How do feature flags, canary releases, and automated rollback reduce deployment risk?

30. **Infrastructure as code and GitOps**  
    What are the benefits and risks of managing infrastructure and deployment state through version-controlled automation?

31. **DevSecOps and shifting security throughout development**  
    Which security checks belong in design, coding, testing, deployment, and operation?

32. **Software supply-chain security**  
    How do dependency attacks, compromised build systems, SBOMs, signed artifacts, and provenance affect software trust?

33. **Zero-trust architecture**

34. **Threat modeling for software projects**  
    How can teams identify meaningful threats early enough to change the design?

35. **Privacy engineering by design**  
    How can data minimization, retention limits, consent, access controls, and privacy threat models become system requirements?

36. **Testing AI-assisted and nondeterministic systems**  
    How must testing change when the same input may not always produce the same output?

37. **Contract testing and consumer-driven APIs**

38. **Database selection and polyglot persistence**

39. **FinOps and engineering for cloud cost**  
    How can developers make cost visible and treat it as a design and operational constraint?

40. **Legacy modernization**

## Project and Product Management

41. **Choosing between Agile, Scrum, Kanban, and continuous flow**  
    Which practices fit different kinds of work, and why does following a framework not guarantee agility?

42. **Product discovery before development**  
    How can interviews, observation, prototypes, and experiments prevent teams from building the wrong product?

43. **Defining a minimum viable product**  
    How can a team reduce scope while preserving a complete and testable value proposition?

44. **Outcome-based roadmaps versus feature roadmaps**  
    Should teams commit to outputs, user outcomes, dates, or learning goals?

45. **Estimating software work under uncertainty**  
    What are the strengths and weaknesses of story points, time estimates, forecasting, and probabilistic methods?

46. **Managing project risk with assumptions and experiments**  
    How can a team identify its most dangerous assumptions and test them early?

47. **Technical debt as a management decision**  
    How can teams identify, communicate, prioritize, and intentionally accept technical debt?

48. **Architecture decision records**  
    How can lightweight records preserve the context and trade-offs behind important technical decisions?

49. **Code review in the age of AI-generated code**  
    How should review practices change when teams can generate more code than humans can carefully inspect?

50. **Measuring developer productivity responsibly**  
    Why are lines of code and commit counts misleading, and what measures better represent delivery and team health?

51. **The SPACE and DORA frameworks**  
    What can these frameworks reveal about developer experience and software delivery performance, and what can they miss?

52. **Psychological safety and effective technical teams**  
    How does a team's ability to question, disagree, report mistakes, and ask for help affect project outcomes?

53. **Leading blameless incident reviews**  
    How can post-incident analysis produce organizational learning without hiding individual or systemic responsibility?

54. **Build versus buy versus open source**  
    How should teams compare control, speed, cost, security, integration, licensing, and long-term dependency?

55. **Open-source project health and governance**  
    How can an organization assess whether a dependency is sustainable, maintained, secure, and aligned with its needs?

56. **Managing globally distributed and hybrid teams**  
    Which communication, documentation, scheduling, and decision practices help distributed teams succeed?

57. **Stakeholder communication for technical projects**  
    How can teams explain uncertainty, trade-offs, delays, and technical risk to nontechnical stakeholders?

58. **Accessibility as a project requirement**  
    How do accessibility decisions affect scope, design, testing, procurement, and the definition of “done”?

59. **Ethical risk assessment for computing projects**  
    How can a project team identify who benefits, who may be harmed, and what safeguards are required before release?

60. **When should a project be canceled?**  
    What evidence should lead a team to pivot, pause, or stop rather than continue because of sunk cost?

## Possible Presentation Approaches

A presentation may take one of several forms:

- **Explainer:** Teach the central ideas with a small concrete example.
- **Demonstration:** Show a tool or technique and critically evaluate the result.
- **Comparison:** Evaluate two or more approaches using explicit decision criteria.
- **Case study:** Analyze a real success, failure, breach, outage, or project decision.
- **Debate:** Present competing positions and defend a reasoned conclusion.
- **Applied recommendation:** Explain whether and how the class should use the topic in a Capping project.

Whatever format you choose, conclude with practical guidance for student project teams: **When should we use this idea, when should we avoid it, and what should we investigate next?**
